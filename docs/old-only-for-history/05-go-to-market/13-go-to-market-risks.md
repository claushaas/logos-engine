# Go-to-Market Risks

## Risk Objective

This document identifies GTM risks that could cause LOGOS Engine to launch with misleading claims, weak learning, poor activation, or premature commercialization.

The goal is to make risks explicit before scaling channels, pricing, or market claims.

## Risk Taxonomy

Risk categories:

- Market risk.
- Segment risk.
- Positioning risk.
- Messaging risk.
- Channel risk.
- Launch risk.
- Funnel risk.
- Sales risk.
- Pricing risk.
- Asset risk.
- Growth metric risk.
- Competitive risk.
- Trust risk.
- Operational GTM risk.

## Risk Scoring Model

Use a simple scoring model:

- Impact: low, medium, high.
- Likelihood: low, medium, high.
- Status: open, monitoring, mitigated, accepted.

High-impact and high-likelihood risks should block GTM scale until mitigation exists.

## Market Risks

The main market risk is that the founder's personal pain does not generalize to enough external users.

Mitigation: conduct discovery, soft launch with qualified builders, and require actual workflow completion before treating feedback as validation.

## Segment Risks

The primary segment may be too narrow, too small, or interested but not activated. Secondary segments may require collaboration, support, or onboarding capabilities that the MVP does not have.

Mitigation: keep the first audience narrow, track segment-specific activation, and delay expansion until usage evidence exists.

## Positioning Risks

Positioning may sound unfamiliar or too broad. Users may not understand whether LOGOS Engine is documentation, AI, product strategy, developer tooling, or project management.

Mitigation: test category language and anchor positioning in concrete workflow examples.

## Messaging Risks

Messaging may overpromise, sound too abstract, or imply that the product validates ideas.

Mitigation: use approved claim boundaries, concrete demos, and explicit "what this is not" language.

## Channel Risks

Channels may produce low-quality attention, hostile feedback, or vanity metrics without activation.

Mitigation: start with small experiments, measure qualified activation, and prioritize channels where users can provide detailed feedback.

## Launch Risks

Launch may occur before installation, provider setup, output structure, examples, or docs are ready.

Mitigation: enforce launch readiness gates and pause launch if core workflow trust is at risk.

## Conversion Funnel Risks

Users may drop off at README comprehension, installation, provider setup, first run, decision review, or interpretation of generated docs.

Mitigation: track drop-off qualitatively, improve onboarding, and make demo assets more concrete.

## Sales Risks

Founder-led conversations may produce false confidence. Friendly users may praise the concept without using it.

Mitigation: separate compliments from behavior, ask for real project trials, and follow up after usage.

## Pricing Risks

Pricing may be invented before value is proven. Charging too early could reduce learning, confuse open-source trust, or optimize for the wrong value metric.

Mitigation: keep MVP free/open-source and run pricing research only after repeat value appears.

## Marketing Asset Risks

Assets may show outdated product behavior, hide setup complexity, or fail to show real outputs.

Mitigation: verify assets against current product behavior and refresh them after workflow changes.

## Growth Metric Risks

The team may overvalue GitHub stars, likes, or views and undervalue completed clarification and user-reported clarity.

Mitigation: use activation and feedback quality as primary learning metrics.

## Competitive Risks

Users may choose generic AI chat, templates, Notion, project management tools, or immediate coding. Competitors may also add structured planning features.

Mitigation: differentiate around local-first state, reviewable decisions, repository-native output, and derived agent context.

## Trust Risks

Trust risks include privacy concerns, AI provider confusion, hidden telemetry concerns, unclear local boundaries, and fear of sensitive project data exposure.

Mitigation: document local-first behavior, provider setup, storage rules, and telemetry boundaries clearly.

## Operational GTM Risks

The project may not have enough founder time for support, feedback synthesis, asset maintenance, and community response after launch.

Mitigation: stage the launch, keep cohorts small, and avoid broad promotion before support readiness.

## Mitigation Plan

Mitigation priorities:

1. Keep claims validation-safe.
2. Launch in small cohorts.
3. Show concrete workflow artifacts.
4. Track activation before reach.
5. Keep pricing deferred.
6. Review risks after each launch wave.

## Accepted Risks

Accepted MVP risks:

- The initial segment is based on founder experience.
- Market demand is not validated.
- Pricing is intentionally deferred.
- Metrics may be manual and qualitative.
- Launch learning may invalidate current positioning.

These risks are acceptable only if they are communicated clearly and not hidden behind confident GTM language.

## Risk Escalation

Escalate GTM risk when:

- Messaging implies unsupported validation.
- Users misunderstand the product's purpose.
- Setup blockers dominate feedback.
- Generated docs create trust concerns.
- A channel produces attention without qualified usage.
- Pricing work starts before value evidence exists.

## Risk Review Cadence

Review GTM risks after each launch cohort and before any broader public push.

The risk register should also be revisited after major product changes, new segment evidence, or new pricing conversations.

## Downstream Handoff

This risk document informs launch decisions, messaging approval, pricing restraint, channel selection, and future validation planning.
