# Market Definition

## Market Definition Summary

LOGOS Engine operates in an emerging intersection between local-first developer tools, AI-assisted product clarification, documentation systems, and decision-first project planning.

The market should be treated as unvalidated. The current definition is a discovery frame that helps decide whom to talk to, what alternatives to compare against, and which claims to avoid.

## Market Category

The working category is a local-first structured intent documentation tool.

Alternative category frames include decision-first documentation engine, repository-native product clarification tool, and AI-assisted project definition TUI. The category should avoid sounding like a generic AI writer, no-code builder, project management suite, or SaaS workspace.

## Market Boundaries

LOGOS Engine belongs near tools that help users define what to build and why before execution begins. It does not belong in categories centered on task tracking, code generation, product analytics, customer research automation, or generic note-taking.

Its boundary is the transition from ambiguous intent to structured, inspectable project documentation.

## Primary Market

The primary market is technical and product-adjacent builders working on early-stage software projects in repository-based workflows.

This includes builders who personally own both product thinking and implementation direction, and who need documentation that can guide humans and agents inside the same project context.

## Market Sizing

Market sizing is not available yet. No reliable evidence currently supports TAM, SAM, SOM, revenue projections, or adoption-rate assumptions.

Sizing work should follow discovery, not precede it. The first useful sizing signal is the number of qualified builders who recognize the problem and complete the workflow.

## Secondary Markets

Possible secondary markets include consultants, small agencies, open-source maintainers, educators, accelerator programs, product coaches, and teams using AI coding agents.

These markets should not be prioritized until the primary builder segment shows clearer evidence of need and repeatable activation.

## Existing Alternatives

Existing alternatives include generic AI chat, documentation templates, Notion or Google Docs, README-first planning, product requirement documents, project management tools, whiteboards, consultants, and simply starting to code without structured clarification.

The strongest substitute may be inertia: builders often tolerate ambiguity because writing structured documentation feels slow or artificial.

## Substitute Behaviors

Common substitute behaviors include:

- Asking a chatbot for a PRD or plan.
- Copying a template and filling it loosely.
- Writing scattered notes in existing docs.
- Opening issues or tasks before decisions are clear.
- Starting implementation and letting requirements emerge from code.
- Relying on memory, chat history, or informal conversations.

## Comparison Lens

LOGOS Engine should be compared on decision quality, inspectability, local control, repository fit, structured state, and usefulness of generated artifacts.

It should not compete on volume of generated prose, broad project management breadth, visual workspace polish, or autonomous business judgment.

## Market Maturity

The market is early and fragmented. AI development tools are maturing quickly, but structured pre-build clarification remains loosely served by templates, consultants, and ad hoc AI prompts.

This creates a possible timing advantage, but the advantage is unproven until users show that they want a dedicated tool instead of improving their current prompt or template workflow.

## Timing Rationale

AI-assisted coding increases the cost of unclear instructions because poor intent can now become poor implementation faster. LOGOS Engine's timing thesis is that builders need a disciplined way to make intent explicit before asking humans or agents to execute.

This is a hypothesis, not yet validated market evidence.

## Market Constraints

The market definition must stay narrow enough to test. Broad claims about startups, product teams, enterprise planning, or universal documentation should be avoided until actual segment evidence supports them.

The initial market also assumes users are comfortable running local tooling and reading generated Markdown.

## Evidence Quality

Evidence quality is currently low for market demand, willingness to pay, retention, and competitive displacement.

Existing evidence is mainly founder-origin experience, internal product reasoning, prior documentation, and hypothesis-level market framing.

## Market Risks

The product may be perceived as a template generator, a wrapper around chat, a documentation chore, or a tool for a problem that users acknowledge but do not prioritize.

Market risk should be managed through interviews, soft-launch usage, comparative demos, and careful tracking of what users do after first generation.

## Downstream Handoff

This market definition informs positioning, messaging, segments, channels, launch order, pricing caution, and GTM risk tracking.
