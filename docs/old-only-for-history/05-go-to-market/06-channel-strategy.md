# Channel Strategy

## Channel Objective

The channel objective is to reach qualified builders who can understand a repository-native local tool, try it in a real project, and provide useful validation feedback.

Channels should prioritize signal quality over volume.

## Channel Thesis

The strongest early channels are likely open-source, technical content, founder-led outreach, and builder communities. These channels match the product's local-first and documentation-first nature better than paid acquisition.

The initial channel thesis is unvalidated and should be tested through small experiments.

## Channel Taxonomy

Channel categories include:

- Owned channels: README, website, docs, project examples, changelog.
- Community channels: GitHub, Hacker News, Reddit, Indie Hackers, developer Discords.
- Content channels: technical essays, demo videos, walkthroughs, comparison posts.
- Social channels: X/Twitter, LinkedIn, personal founder network.
- Direct channels: founder-led outreach and live demos.
- Partnership channels: consultants, educators, open-source maintainers.

## Primary Channel

The primary channel should be GitHub plus technical content. GitHub gives the product a natural home, while technical content explains the problem and shows the workflow.

The first goal is not stars alone. It is to convert qualified readers into local installation, workflow completion, and feedback.

## Secondary Channels

Secondary channels include Hacker News, Reddit communities, Indie Hackers, LinkedIn, X/Twitter, newsletters, open-source directories, and small private communities of builders.

Each channel should be tested with a tailored message and a specific learning goal.

## Organic Channels

Organic channels should include README search, blog posts, documentation pages, example repositories, demo recordings, and public changelog updates.

Organic content should answer concrete questions: what the product does, why it is local-first, what output it generates, and what it does not validate.

## Paid Channels

Paid channels should be deferred. There is no current evidence that paid acquisition would produce qualified users or that conversion economics are viable.

Paid experiments can be revisited after activation, messaging, and segment fit are better understood.

## Community Channels

Community channels are useful for feedback, but they require care. Posts should be transparent about the product's early stage and ask for the right type of feedback.

The best community motion is a demo-oriented ask: "Does this workflow help you clarify a real project before building?"

## Partnership Channels

Possible partners include product coaches, technical educators, consultants, agency operators, and open-source maintainers who teach or practice structured project definition.

Partnerships should stay informal until segment value is demonstrated.

## Sales Channels

Sales channels should be founder-led and discovery-oriented. The purpose is to learn from high-fit builders, not to close revenue.

Any sales-style conversation should produce evidence about pain, alternatives, objections, activation, and willingness to continue using the tool.

## Marketplace and Platform Channels

Marketplace or platform channels are later-stage options. Potential future channels include package registries, open-source directories, AI tooling directories, developer tool lists, and agent workflow ecosystems.

They should not be the first bet unless the product has a clear self-serve activation path.

## Segment-Channel Fit

Solo builders fit GitHub, Hacker News, Indie Hackers, Reddit, and demo content.

Product-minded engineers fit technical essays, GitHub, LinkedIn, and engineering communities.

Consultants and agencies fit direct outreach, demos, referrals, and practical before/after examples.

Open-source maintainers fit GitHub, project examples, contribution guides, and maintainer communities.

## Message-Channel Fit

GitHub should emphasize installation, local-first architecture, and examples.

Technical essays should explain the problem: faster execution increases the need for clearer intent.

Community posts should ask for feedback and show concrete output.

Social posts should use short before/after narratives and link to demos.

## Channel Assets

Required channel assets include:

- GitHub README.
- Installation instructions.
- Short terminal demo.
- Sample repository.
- Before/after documentation tree.
- Claim-safe landing page.
- Founder launch post.
- Feedback form or issue template.

## Channel Economics

Channel economics are not validated. Early economics should be measured in founder time, qualified conversations, activation rate, and evidence quality.

Cost-per-acquisition and paid payback metrics are not relevant until a paid model exists.

## Channel Experiments

Initial experiments:

- GitHub README plus demo: measure installs and issues.
- Founder-led outreach to qualified builders: measure replies and completed trials.
- Technical essay: measure qualified visits and conversations.
- Community post with demo: measure clarity of objections.
- Sample project walkthrough: measure whether users understand the generated artifacts.

## Channel Metrics

Useful channel metrics include:

- Qualified visits.
- GitHub stars and watches.
- Installation attempts.
- Completed local runs.
- Generated documentation sets.
- Feedback submissions.
- Discovery calls.
- Repeat use signals.
- Contribution interest.

## Channel Constraints

Channels must not rely on untrue claims. Messaging must disclose that demand and pricing are not validated.

The product also has a technical channel constraint: users must be comfortable with local installation, repository access, and AI provider configuration.

## Fallback Strategy

If broad community channels produce low-quality attention, fallback to narrow founder-led discovery with carefully selected builders.

If GitHub interest does not convert into usage, prioritize onboarding, examples, and demo clarity before adding channels.

## Channel Risks

Risks include audience mismatch, abstract messaging, low conversion from curiosity to usage, noisy community feedback, and launch attention that does not produce learning.

## Downstream Handoff

This strategy informs launch execution, funnel instrumentation, marketing assets, growth metrics, and GTM risk monitoring.
