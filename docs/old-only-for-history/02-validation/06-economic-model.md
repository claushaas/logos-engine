# Economic Model

This document evaluates whether LOGOS Engine could plausibly pay its own costs and generate enough margin to continue existing under explicit assumptions.

The current answer is provisional:

> LOGOS Engine is **not yet economically proven**. A plausible economic model exists only if the project first validates that users experience meaningful pre-execution ambiguity, that structured documentation creates observable clarity, and that at least one audience will exchange money, time, trust, or workflow change for that clarity.

No revenue, pricing, conversion, churn, CAC, LTV, margin, or market-size claims are validated yet. All numbers in this document are placeholders, formulas, or assumptions until evidence is collected.

## Economic Validation Objective

The economic objective is to determine whether LOGOS Engine can sustain itself without violating its Foundation boundaries: local-first operation, user-owned decisions, inspectable files, no hidden remote-first state, no surprise telemetry, no false authority, and no pressure toward paid services that undermine user agency.

The minimum viable economic outcome for the current stage is not venture-scale growth. It is evidence that the project can support continued development and operation while preserving the local-first structured-documentation model.

The current economic question is:

> Is there a plausible configuration where enough users or buyers value reduced ambiguity, better scoping, clearer decisions, or reusable structured documentation enough to cover the project's ongoing costs and leave a margin for maintenance?

### Stage-Specific Economic Goal

| Stage | Economic Goal | Evidence Needed | Decision Informed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Validation | Identify plausible value drivers and cost structure. | Problem severity, workflow value, trust fit, willingness-to-pay signals after value is demonstrated. | Decide whether economic validation should proceed. |
| Early product | Cover direct operating costs and some maintainer time. | Real usage, paid interest, support burden, API/tooling cost, setup friction. | Decide whether to package, charge, sponsor, or keep purely community-funded. |
| Sustainable project | Fund ongoing maintenance, support, profile quality, documentation, and infrastructure without boundary violations. | Revenue, retention, cost, contribution, and support data. | Decide whether the model is sustainable. |

### Minimum Viable Economic Outcome

For the project to continue beyond validation, one of these must become plausible:

- **User-paid model:** individual users pay because the workflow reduces ambiguity, rework, scoping effort, or documentation inconsistency.
- **Buyer-paid model:** consultants, agencies, teams, or organizations pay because structured documentation reduces scoping cost, improves client intake, or increases project readiness.
- **Open-source sustainability model:** free local usage remains core, while donations, sponsors, grants, support, services, or optional hosted offerings fund maintenance.
- **Hybrid model:** the core remains open and local-first, while paid value exists around convenience, services, support, hosted opt-in features, team workflows, or advanced profile support.

The model fails economically if the only way to monetize is to create lock-in, force cloud state, hide AI/data movement, sell false certainty, or turn LOGOS Engine into a generic execution platform.

### Value Metric Candidates

Possible value metrics:

- Number of active local workspaces.
- Number of maintained project profiles.
- Number of users or seats in a team.
- Number of client intake projects for consultants or agencies.
- Support level or response time.
- Hosted opt-in convenience usage, if validated later.
- Advanced profile or validation-pack access.

Value metrics to avoid:

- Number of generated documents, because document volume is a false success signal.
- Number of AI messages, because conversation length does not prove clarity.
- Amount of project data uploaded, because this conflicts with privacy and Local First.
- Lock-in-based metrics that penalize export, local ownership, or inspectability.

### Pricing Evidence Needed

Pricing should wait for evidence that:

- Users experience the problem as costly.
- The workflow creates observable clarity.
- Users can connect clarity to reduced rework, faster scoping, better handoff, or lower risk.
- Users understand what they would pay for: core tool, profiles, support, hosted convenience, team workflows, or services.
- A payer or budget owner can be identified.

Compliments, signups, stars, curiosity, or demo praise are not willingness-to-pay evidence.

## Unit Economics

Unit economics cannot be calculated yet because price, conversion, churn, CAC, support burden, usage cost, and retention are unknown. This section defines the formulas and the variables that must later be filled with evidence.

### Core Formulas

```text
Gross Margin = (Revenue - Direct Cost of Service) / Revenue

Contribution Margin = Revenue - Variable Costs - Direct Support Cost - Payment Fees

Monthly Contribution = Paying Customers * Average Revenue per Customer * Contribution Margin %

Break-even Customers = Fixed Monthly Costs / Average Contribution per Customer

LTV = Average Revenue per Customer * Gross Margin % * Average Customer Lifetime

CAC = Sales and Marketing Cost / New Paying Customers

Payback Period = CAC / Average Monthly Contribution per Customer

LTV:CAC = LTV / CAC
```

### Metric Status

| Metric | Formula | Current Value | Evidence Level | Interpretation | Risk If Wrong | Update Cadence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Average revenue per customer | Revenue / paying customers | unknown | none | Depends on pricing and packaging. | Break-even may be impossible if price is too low. | After pricing tests. |
| Direct cost of service | AI/API + hosting + payment fees + direct operational cost | unknown | none | Local-first core may reduce hosting cost, but support and AI costs remain. | Gross margin may be overstated. | Monthly once active. |
| Gross margin | (Revenue - direct cost) / revenue | unknown | none | Cannot be claimed until paid usage exists. | Low margin could invalidate paid support-heavy or usage-heavy models. | Monthly once active. |
| Contribution margin | Revenue - variable cost - direct support | unknown | none | More relevant than gross margin if support is heavy. | Hidden support labor may make the model non-viable. | Monthly once active. |
| CAC | acquisition spend / new paying customers | unknown | none | Early organic/open-source adoption may not translate to paid acquisition. | Paid growth may not work. | Per channel later. |
| LTV | ARPC * gross margin * lifetime | unknown | none | Retention must be validated first. | One-time use can make subscriptions weak. | After retention evidence. |
| Payback period | CAC / monthly contribution | unknown | none | Cannot be calculated before CAC and contribution are known. | Long payback may require funding or low-cost distribution. | Later. |
| LTV:CAC | LTV / CAC | unknown | none | Not meaningful before paid acquisition evidence. | Can create false precision. | Later. |

### Unit Economic Implications

The economic model is most likely to work if:

- Users return because decisions change, documents need refresh, or new projects need foundations.
- AI/API cost is either low, user-paid through provider configuration, local, or explicitly included in pricing.
- Support burden is controlled through good onboarding, documentation, diagnostics, and simple local setup.
- Paid value maps to clarity, reduced rework, reusable profiles, support, or team/client intake outcomes rather than document volume.
- Acquisition can happen through open-source, content, referrals, or communities without requiring high paid CAC.

The model is weak if:

- Users treat LOGOS Engine as a one-time document generator.
- Support needs rise faster than revenue.
- Users require hosted collaboration before paying.
- Paid value depends on hiding local-first complexity instead of making it understandable.
- AI/API usage becomes a margin sink or privacy liability.

## Break-even Analysis

Break-even cannot be calculated with actual values yet. The model should use formulas and scenario inputs until evidence exists.

### Break-even Variables

| Variable | Meaning | Current Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F | Fixed monthly costs: maintainer time target, tooling, infrastructure, admin, documentation, support baseline. | unknown |
| P | Average monthly price per paying customer or account. | unknown |
| V | Variable monthly cost per customer: AI/API, hosting, payment fees, direct support, usage-based costs. | unknown |
| CM | Contribution margin per customer: P - V. | unknown |
| N | Paying customers or accounts required to break even. | unknown |
| CR | Conversion rate from lead/free/trial/community user to paid. | unknown |
| L | Leads or active free users required to produce N paying customers. | unknown |

### Break-even Formulas

```text
Contribution Margin per Customer = P - V

Break-even Paying Customers = F / (P - V)

Required Leads or Free Users = Break-even Paying Customers / Conversion Rate

Cash Break-even = Monthly Revenue - Monthly Cash Costs >= 0

Maintenance Break-even = Monthly Revenue - Monthly Cash Costs - Target Maintainer Compensation >= 0
```

### Break-even Interpretation

There are two separate break-even questions:

- **Cash break-even:** Can direct cash costs be covered?
- **Maintenance break-even:** Can the project also compensate enough labor to keep development, support, documentation, and profile quality sustainable?

Cash break-even may be easy for a local-first open-source tool with low infrastructure costs. Maintenance break-even is the harder and more honest target, because founder or maintainer labor is the largest likely early cost.

Break-even becomes unrealistic if:

- P is low and V or support cost is high.
- Conversion from free/open-source usage to paid support or paid features is weak.
- Users only need the product once.
- The project must add hosted collaboration or enterprise support before revenue exists.
- Acquisition requires paid channels before unit economics are known.

### Maximum Tolerable Thresholds

These thresholds are qualitative until metrics exist:

- Support burden is too high if each user requires custom help that cannot be reduced through documentation, defaults, or product changes.
- AI/API cost is too high if normal usage cannot be covered by pricing, user-provided providers, or local operation.
- CAC is too high if acquisition requires paid spend before LTV and contribution margin are known.
- Churn is too high if users do not return after the first documentation package unless the model is intentionally one-time or service-based.
- Price is too low if it covers compute but not maintenance, support, profile quality, or documentation.

### Revision, Pivot, Stop

**Revise** if value exists but pricing, packaging, or support burden is wrong.

**Pivot** if the payer, buyer, or sustainable segment differs from the initial user, such as agencies or teams paying for repeatable intake.

**Stop or reduce scope** if the only viable revenue path requires violating Local First, user ownership, transparent AI boundaries, or the project's identity as a documentation system rather than an execution platform.

## Economic Validation Summary

The economic model is **not yet proven**.

There is a plausible sustainability path, but only under conditions that remain unvalidated:

- The problem must be costly enough that users care before being sold the solution.
- Structured documentation must create observable clarity beyond generic AI prose or ad hoc notes.
- At least one early audience must tolerate the local-first workflow and setup friction.
- Users or buyers must connect clarity to avoided cost, reduced rework, better scoping, improved handoff, or repeated use.
- Costs, especially founder labor, support, AI/API usage, profile maintenance, and documentation, must remain lower than the contribution created by the revenue model.
