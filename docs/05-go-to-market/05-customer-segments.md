# Customer Segments

## Segmentation Objective

Segmentation should identify which builders are most likely to feel the problem, tolerate the current product shape, complete the local workflow, and provide useful validation feedback.

The goal is learning, not broad coverage.

## Segment Taxonomy

Segments should be organized by workflow fit:

- Repository-native solo builders.
- Technical founders and indie hackers.
- Product-minded engineers.
- Consultants and small agencies.
- Open-source maintainers.
- Educators and mentors.
- Early teams using AI coding agents.

## Segment Overview

The strongest initial segments combine three traits: they own project direction, they can run local developer tooling, and they have experienced the cost of unclear pre-build decisions.

Segments that need visual collaboration, procurement, team administration, or non-technical onboarding are likely later-stage segments.

## Ideal Customer Profile

The ideal early customer profile is a builder who:

- Works in Git-backed repositories.
- Uses Markdown or structured docs.
- Is comfortable with CLI or TUI workflows.
- Has started projects from unclear intent before.
- Wants better context for implementation or AI agents.
- Is willing to give detailed feedback on an early product.

## Primary Segment

The primary segment is technical/product-adjacent solo builders working on app-business ideas, internal tools, open-source projects, or early product concepts.

This segment is closest to the founder-origin experience and should receive the first validation focus.

## Secondary Segments

Secondary segments include:

- Consultants who need clearer project baselines.
- Small agencies that create client discovery outputs.
- Open-source maintainers defining project direction.
- Product coaches or educators teaching structured project thinking.
- Small teams adopting AI-assisted implementation.

## Early Adopters

Early adopters are likely builders who already write docs, maintain repositories, experiment with AI tooling, and publicly discuss product process or engineering clarity.

They should be recruited for qualitative feedback before broad launch.

## Role Map

In the earliest segment, one person often holds multiple roles: user, project owner, buyer, decision maker, and champion.

In later team contexts, these roles may separate into founder, product lead, engineering lead, consultant, client sponsor, and implementation team.

## Users

Users are the people who run the TUI, answer clarification prompts, review proposed decisions, and generate documentation artifacts.

They need the workflow to be understandable, trustworthy, and fast enough to fit before implementation.

## Buyers and Payers

Buyers and payers are not validated yet. In the MVP, there may be no payer because the product should be open-source or free-to-use while validation continues.

Future payers may include founders, consultants, agencies, teams, or organizations buying support, hosted workflow, profile packs, or commercial licensing.

## Decision Makers

For early users, the decision maker is the builder deciding whether to install and try the tool.

For later organizational use, decision makers may care about trust, privacy, repeatability, documentation quality, and integration with existing development workflows.

## Champions, Influencers, and Blockers

Champions may be product-minded engineers, founders, consultants, and AI workflow enthusiasts.

Influencers may include open-source maintainers, technical writers, product coaches, and developer community leaders.

Blockers may include users who dislike terminal tools, teams standardized on SaaS workspaces, or stakeholders who view documentation as overhead.

## Qualification and Disqualification

Qualified early users:

- Have an active or recent project to clarify.
- Use repositories and Markdown.
- Can install local tools.
- Are willing to review generated docs critically.

Disqualified early users:

- Need a non-technical web-only experience.
- Want market validation automation.
- Want a project management suite.
- Are not willing to review or edit proposed decisions.

## Segment Needs

Primary segment needs include fast setup, clear demo value, strong examples, trustworthy local operation, claim-safe AI behavior, and documentation that helps with the next implementation step.

Secondary segments may need collaboration, repeatable client workflows, profile customization, brandable outputs, and stronger support materials.

## Segment-Specific Messaging

Solo builders: "Turn a rough idea into clearer project docs before you code."

Technical founders: "Make your product intent explicit before implementation accelerates."

Consultants: "Create a structured project baseline before execution or handoff."

Open-source maintainers: "Use a repeatable local workflow to define direction, risks, and contribution context."

## Segment-Specific Channels

Solo builders and indie hackers: Indie Hackers, X/Twitter, Hacker News, GitHub, personal networks.

Product-minded engineers: technical blogs, GitHub, Reddit, LinkedIn, engineering communities.

Consultants and agencies: founder-led outreach, case-study content, referrals, and direct demos.

Open-source maintainers: GitHub, open-source directories, maintainer communities, and contribution examples.

## Segment Prioritization

Prioritize the primary solo builder segment first because it has the clearest workflow fit and lowest buying complexity.

Secondary segments should be explored only after the product demonstrates repeatable activation with the primary segment.

## Excluded Segments

Excluded initial segments include large enterprises, non-technical founders needing a fully hosted UI, procurement-heavy organizations, teams requiring collaboration controls, and users seeking automated business validation.

## Expansion Triggers

Expansion should be considered when:

- Multiple users in one secondary segment request the same workflow.
- Primary users repeatedly ask to share outputs with collaborators.
- Consultants use the product in client-facing work.
- Users ask for profile customization, team review, or paid support.

## Segment Risks

The main risk is selecting users who like the idea intellectually but do not complete the workflow. Another risk is confusing personal founder pain with a wider market need.

Segment validation should track actual usage, not only positive feedback.

## Downstream Handoff

This segmentation informs channels, launch cohorts, messaging variants, sales discovery, pricing hypotheses, and growth metrics.
