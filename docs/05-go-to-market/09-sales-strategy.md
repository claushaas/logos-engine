# Sales Strategy

## Sales Objective

The sales objective is discovery, learning, and qualified activation. LOGOS Engine should not start with a revenue sales motion because pricing and willingness to pay are not validated.

Founder-led conversations should help identify who feels the problem, what alternatives they use, and whether the product creates enough value to continue.

## Sales Motion Thesis

The initial thesis is that self-serve open-source usage plus founder-led discovery is the right early motion.

Sales should feel like product learning: show the workflow, listen for pain, observe whether the builder tries it, and follow up on outcomes.

## Sales Motion Taxonomy

Potential motions:

- Self-serve open-source adoption.
- Product-led activation through README and demo.
- Founder-led discovery and demos.
- Consultant or agency referral.
- Future sales-led motion for teams, if validated.

## Self-Serve Motion

Self-serve is the default motion. A qualified builder should be able to understand the product, install it, run it locally, and generate docs without a sales call.

Self-serve success depends on excellent examples and clear onboarding.

## Product-Led Motion

The product-led motion is local activation. The user experiences value by completing a clarification cycle and seeing useful documentation appear in the repository.

Product-led growth cannot be claimed until repeatable activation and retention signals exist.

## Founder-Led Sales

Founder-led sales should be used for discovery calls, demos, soft-launch recruitment, and high-quality feedback.

The founder should ask about current workflow, recent project ambiguity, alternatives tried, trust concerns, and whether the generated output changes the next step.

## Sales-Led Motion

A sales-led motion is not appropriate for the MVP. It may become relevant later if teams, agencies, or organizations show willingness to pay for support, collaboration, hosted workflow, or commercial licensing.

## Hybrid Motion

A future hybrid motion could combine open-source self-serve adoption with paid support, team features, hosted collaboration, or specialized profile packs.

This remains a hypothesis.

## Buyer and User Roles

In the early market, buyer and user are usually the same person.

In future team settings, users may be builders or product leads, while buyers may be founders, agency owners, engineering managers, or client sponsors.

## Qualification Criteria

Qualified sales/discovery targets:

- Own an active project.
- Have felt project ambiguity recently.
- Work in repositories.
- Can run local tooling.
- Are open to AI-assisted but review-based workflows.
- Can provide specific feedback after trying the product.

## Lead Scoring

Early lead scoring should be simple:

- High: active project, local workflow fit, clear pain, willingness to try.
- Medium: clear pain but weak technical fit or no immediate project.
- Low: curiosity only, no repository workflow, or expectation of business validation automation.

## Sales Process

Suggested process:

1. Identify qualified builder.
2. Ask about recent project ambiguity.
3. Show the local workflow and sample output.
4. Invite trial on a real or sample project.
5. Follow up on what became clearer and what blocked usage.
6. Capture evidence in validation notes.

## Demo Strategy

The demo should be short and concrete. It should show a repository before LOGOS Engine, the TUI workflow, reviewed decisions, generated Markdown in `logos/`, and derived HTML or agent pack output.

The demo should avoid abstract diagrams without showing files.

## Objection Handling

"I already use AI chat." Emphasize structured state, review, repeatability, and repository-native output.

"I do not want more docs." Emphasize decisions, assumptions, risks, and implementation context, not document volume.

"This seems too early." Agree, then ask for feedback on the core workflow and pain.

"Can it validate my business?" No. It can clarify the project, but external validation remains separate.

## Sales Assets

Useful sales assets:

- Demo script.
- Sample project.
- Before/after docs.
- Objection response sheet.
- One-page product overview.
- Feedback guide.
- Founder discovery question list.

## Sales Metrics

Early metrics:

- Qualified conversations.
- Demo-to-trial rate.
- Trial-to-activation rate.
- Feedback quality.
- Repeat use intent.
- Objection frequency.
- Segment fit notes.

## Sales Handoffs

Sales/discovery insights should feed product docs, messaging, segment prioritization, onboarding improvements, and risk tracking.

There is no revenue operations handoff yet.

## Sales Constraints

Sales must not imply validated demand, ROI, product-market fit, or business success.

The product also cannot support enterprise expectations such as admin controls, hosted collaboration, SLAs, or procurement workflows in the MVP.

## Sales Risks

Risks include treating positive calls as validation, selling future capabilities, overfitting to one friendly user, or confusing consulting interest with scalable product demand.

## Downstream Handoff

This sales strategy informs launch cohorts, pricing caution, funnel stages, marketing assets, and GTM risk review.
