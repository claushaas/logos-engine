# Pricing and Packaging

## Pricing Objective

The pricing objective is to preserve validation flexibility while avoiding premature monetization claims. Pricing should not be introduced until users demonstrate repeated value, activation, retention, and willingness to pay.

## Pricing Thesis

The current pricing thesis is that LOGOS Engine should launch as open-source or free-to-use while its core value is validated.

Future pricing may be attached to support, hosted workflows, profile packs, team collaboration, commercial licensing, or services, but these are unvalidated hypotheses.

## Value Metric

Potential future value metrics include:

- Number of active projects clarified.
- Number of team members or collaborators.
- Advanced profiles or specialized packs.
- Hosted workspaces or sync.
- Support level.
- Commercial usage rights.

No value metric is validated yet.

## Pricing Model

The MVP pricing model should be free/open-source.

Possible future models:

- Sponsorships or donations.
- Paid support.
- Commercial license for organizations.
- Paid hosted collaboration.
- Paid profile packs.
- Consulting or implementation services.

## Packaging Strategy

Initial packaging should be simple:

- Core engine.
- Initial Standard profile.
- Local TUI workflow.
- Markdown generation.
- Derived HTML artifacts.
- Derived agent packs.
- Documentation and examples.

Avoid artificial limits in the MVP that would interfere with learning.

## Plan Structure

No paid plan structure should be launched initially.

A future structure could include:

- Free open-source core.
- Pro support or advanced profiles.
- Team collaboration or hosted workspace.
- Enterprise/commercial licensing.

This structure requires validation before implementation.

## Feature and Limit Mapping

Initial feature mapping:

- Core local TUI: free/open-source.
- Initial Standard profile: free/open-source.
- Generated Markdown: free/open-source.
- HTML artifacts and agent packs: free/open-source.
- Custom profiles, hosted collaboration, support, or commercial terms: future hypotheses.

## Free Tier Strategy

The free tier is the product for the MVP. Its purpose is validation, adoption, contribution, and trust.

The free tier should not hide core local value behind payment.

## Trial Strategy

No trial is needed for the open-source MVP.

Future paid trials may be relevant for hosted or team offerings.

## Subscription Terms

Subscription terms are deferred.

No subscription promise should appear in marketing materials until a paid product exists.

## Payment Flow

Payment flow is deferred.

Engineering should not prioritize payment infrastructure until pricing evidence justifies it.

## Tax and Invoice Considerations

Tax and invoice handling are not in scope for MVP GTM.

They become relevant only if paid plans, support contracts, hosted services, or commercial licensing are introduced.

## Discount Strategy

Discount strategy is deferred.

Discounting before price validation would create noise instead of insight.

## Upgrade and Downgrade Logic

Upgrade and downgrade logic is deferred.

Future logic should be designed only after packaging and value metrics are validated.

## Cancellation Logic

Cancellation logic is deferred because there is no paid subscription.

If a future hosted or paid product exists, cancellation should be simple and transparent.

## Refund Policy

Refund policy is deferred until payment exists.

Any future policy should prioritize trust and avoid dark patterns.

## Pricing Objections

Expected future objections:

- "Why pay if the core is open-source?"
- "Is this different from a prompt?"
- "Does this save enough time?"
- "Is the value in docs, process, profiles, or support?"

These objections should be explored before pricing is finalized.

## Pricing Experiments

Pricing experiments should wait until users show repeated value.

Possible later experiments:

- Willingness-to-pay interviews.
- Paid support pilot.
- Commercial license conversations.
- Hosted workflow waitlist.
- Profile-pack demand test.

## Pricing Metrics

Future pricing metrics:

- Activated users.
- Repeat usage.
- Support demand.
- Paid intent.
- Conversion to paid pilot.
- Retention by package.
- Churn reasons.

These metrics are not available yet.

## Pricing Risks

The main pricing risk is monetizing before value is proven. Other risks include charging for the wrong value metric, undermining open-source trust, and overbuilding billing infrastructure.

## Downstream Handoff

This pricing strategy informs GTM messaging, sales conversations, roadmap prioritization, and future business model validation.
